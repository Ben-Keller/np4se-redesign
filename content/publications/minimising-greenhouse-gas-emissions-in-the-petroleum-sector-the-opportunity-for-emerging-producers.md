<p><strong>The importance of limiting greenhouse gas emissions</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Limiting climate change requires urgent, significant reductions of GHG emissions by the petroleum sector</li>
<li>The bulk of emissions relate to the end use of petroleum products, and are spread among many users, these are difficult to reduce</li>
<li>Emissions from upstream production of oil and gas are concentrated in producing countries that can act to have a significant impact by reducing them</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>The Opportunity for New Producers: To take the lead and protect their projects from stranding</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Changing an established petroleum sector, designed for different standards, is very difficult</li>
<li>New producers have the opportunity to design their laws, regulatory systems, monitoring regimes and projects to minimise GHG emissions.</li>
<li>Emissions intensities vary widely; using existing technologies and best practice can enable new producers to be at the low end of those ranges</li>
<li>Doing so should make their projects less likely to become stranded as climate change related restrictions tighten</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Minimising GHG emissions should be at the centre of all decisions about the sector</strong></p>
<p>From initial project design =&gt; Through operating practices =&gt; To a fully funded decommissioning plan</p>
<p><strong>The paper discusses</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>The levers that governments have at their disposal</li>
<li>The challenges governments face in moving from pledges to implementation</li>
<li>Suggests a framework for decision making and to support low GHG projects</li>
<li>Makes recommendations in each of the key areas</li>
<li>Provides links to further resources</li>
</ul>
